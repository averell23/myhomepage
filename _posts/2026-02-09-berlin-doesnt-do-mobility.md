Sad news for Berlin: The senate wants to [push maglev trains](https://www.spiegel.de/auto/berlin-senat-favorisiert-magnetbahn-von-spandau-zu-stillgelegtem-flughafen-tegel-a-a5420a4b-a804-4ac1-95e6-cf9b6b692ebc) for public transport. For this, they are cancelling an already-planned tram line.

Reason? The tram (or even bus lines) would require the removal of parking space. Which is beyond absurd. The city still seems to believe that dedicating public space to car parking should be prioritized over the mobility needs of the inhabitants. And this isn't the only tram line cancelled. Even the previous "red-green" government hardly did anything about the bicycling infrastructure; but maintained that €10.20 are a great price for parking -- **per year**. For reference, London charges up to ￡1000 for some cars. But we'll maybe raise it to €60. Maybe. Sometimes.

The current senate seems utterly incapable of creating a more liveable city; they even started boycotting the inner districts working plans.

The sad part is, history has shown that every time you remove cars from somewhere, people will love it. People will vote for you. Car-free city center in my home town. Walkable banks of the Seine in Paris. Walkable waterfront in San Francisco. Yes, people briefly complained. But after a short while, no one ever wanted to return to the previous state...
